How Red Bull found its straight-line speed in 2026, and why Mercedes lost it

Jatin Garg

Independent analysis of public Formula 1 timing data

10 October 2026

Abstract

The 2026 Formula 1 rules made roughly half of a car's peak power electric and placed it under a per-lap energy budget and a speed-dependent limit. I study how Red Bull Racing closed its early-season deficit, using every session of the season so far: — sessions, — timed laps and — passes along full-throttle straights. Laps are aligned by projecting car positions onto a reference racing line, and race pace is corrected for fuel, tyre age, compound and dirty air. Red Bull's qualifying gap to pole fell from — in rounds 1–5 to — in rounds 12–17. Its corrected race-pace gap fell from — to —. Straights account for — of the — per lap closed on Mercedes. Relative to the field, Red Bull's straight-line time improved while Mercedes' worsened by more. The gain is not top speed: Red Bull's speed-trap reading fell relative to the field, while it gained in the first half of each straight. This is consistent with a strong combustion engine paired with a weaker battery, under rules that from Miami let the engine recharge the battery at full throttle.

Keywords: Formula 1, telemetry, energy management, power units, FastF1

Introduction

In March Red Bull qualified more than a second off pole at Suzuka. By October it had taken consecutive poles at Sepang and Singapore. The paddock explanation is that Red Bull "found straight-line speed" and is now quicker than Mercedes on the straights. That claim is hard to test from speed traps, because in 2026 a car can lose fifty kilometres per hour on a straight while its driver keeps the throttle pressed (Section 4.3).

This note asks three questions. Where on the lap did Red Bull find its time? How much of its straight-line gain is its own, and how much is Mercedes slowing down? And what does the shape of the gain say about the cause? Section 2 summarises the 2026 power-unit rules, Section 3 the data and methods, Section 4 the results and Section 5 the interpretation.

Data and methods

Data

The data are every 2026 session available on 10 October, from Australia to Singapore qualifying. They were downloaded from Formula 1's live-timing archive with FastF1 [6]: lap timing, car telemetry (speed, RPM, gear, throttle, brake) at about 4 Hz, car positions at about 4 Hz, weather and race-control messages. The 2026 Bahrain Grand Prix was held at Sepang and is treated as Sepang throughout. The public feed carries no battery, Overtake or wing-state channel. The legacy DRS field is zero for all of 2026.

Alignment on the racing line

Distance obtained by integrating speed drifts by up to several per cent, and in some sessions the timing and telemetry clocks differ by seconds (21 s in the rain-delayed Sepang race). Instead, for each circuit I build a reference line 𝐫(s) from the position samples of a clean pole lap, densified to 1 m. Every position sample 𝐩i is then mapped to the track coordinate

si= argmins∈[0,L) ‖𝐩i−𝐫(s)‖ (2)

and car-data samples inherit s by interpolation in time. The median sample lies 0.3 m from the reference line. Each driver's clock offset is estimated from the line crossings it implies. Laps are resampled on a 5 m grid.

Straights, corners and clean air

A circuit's straights are the full-throttle zones of its reference lap that are at least 200 m long. Every lap of every car is measured on each one: entry speed, peak, exit, time taken, and speed lost at full throttle. Non-straight track is assigned to the nearest corner and classed by the reference apex speed: slow below 120 km/h, medium 120–200 km/h, fast above that. The clean-air gap g is the time since any other car, lapped or not, crossed the line ahead. A lap is clean when g>2 s.

For each race, green-flag laps on slick tyres, excluding the first lap, in- and out-laps, and laps more than 4% off the median, are fitted by ordinary least squares:

Losing speed at full throttle

Figure 5 shows why speed traps mislead in 2026. On Albert Park's back straight both cars are at full throttle from corner exit to the braking point. Both peak about halfway down and then lose around 50 km/h. With harvest power Ph and mass m, the added deceleration is

Δa≈Phmv (5)

For example, 350 kW at 300 km/h on an 800 kg car gives about 5 m/s², or 19 km/h per second, which is the order of the slopes observed. Every car does this on every long straight, so a single trap reading says little about straight-line performance.

Discussion

The explanations are ordered by how much of the effect each appears to account for.

  1. Mercedes lost its straight-line advantage. Every Mercedes-powered team lost straight-line time relative to the field after round 5. That it happened is well supported. The cause is not established: the compression-ratio test and a draggier aerodynamic package both fit.
  2. Red Bull-Ford learned where to spend its energy. The gain sits at corner exit while trap speed fell, and Racing Bulls shares about two-thirds of it. This points to the shared power unit and its energy strategy, and the post-Miami super-clip rules favour a strong ICE. Confidence: medium-high.
  3. The rotating rear wing and aerodynamic packages. Red Bull gained more than Racing Bulls, and its weakest straight-line weekend came without the wing. Confidence: medium.
  4. A lighter, more driveable car. Weight came off in Miami and Austria, and medium-speed corner losses halved. Confidence: medium.
  5. Better tyre use late in the season. Confidence: low to medium.

Two things did not happen. Red Bull did not gain top speed, and it did not receive ADUO help.

Limitations

Energy use is inferred from speed traces, because the public feed carries no state of charge, Overtake state or wing position. Monaco's race position feed stops halfway and Hungary's is degraded, so both races are excluded from race straight-line measures. Qualifying gaps include driver effects, including Verstappen's Q1 crash in Australia and his engine fault in Baku qualifying. Singapore sprint qualifying is excluded because of the FIA's Overtake-mode error. "Medium confidence" means the data fit an explanation and nothing contradicts it, not that the explanation is proven.
